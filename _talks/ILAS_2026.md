**Abstract.** This talk develops information-theoretic de Finetti methods for constructing convergent approximation hierarchies with explicit finite-level guarantees, including both outer relaxations and certified feasible inner approximations. For polynomial optimization over convex bodies subject to local equality and inequality constraints, an information-theoretic monogamy argument yields a convergent conic hierarchy with approximation error O(n^(−1/2)) at extension level n. This resolves the absence of finite-level error bounds in de Finetti–based methods with inequality constraints. A constructive rounding procedure then converts solutions of the outer relaxations into certified feasible points, producing complementary inner and outer bounds that sandwich the true optimum.

We apply this framework to two-player nonlocal games in generalized probabilistic theories (GPTs), expressing their optimal GPT value as a locally constrained polynomial optimization problem and thereby obtaining quantitative finite-level approximation guarantees. For fixed-size quantum free games with bounded entanglement assistance, we derive semidefinite-programming outer hierarchies with ε-additive guarantees and combine them with measurement-based rounding to generate convergent inner sequences of feasible strategies. By exploiting representation-theoretic symmetry reduction — including Bose symmetry, Schur–Weyl duality, and block decompositions — we formulate the SDPs directly in symmetry-adapted bases and achieve overall complexity polynomial in 1/ε.

Finally, for approximate quantum error correction under symmetric noise, we round extendability-based outer bounds into explicit encoder–decoder pairs. We also develop a computational framework that combines noise symmetry with extendability symmetry through commuting group actions, making higher levels of the hierarchy tractable.
